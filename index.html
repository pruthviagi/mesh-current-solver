How to describe your drawing

Treat every mesh as a rectangle with a top, bottom, left and right side. A branch is a stretch of wire between two junctions that holds resistors or batteries. Plain wires can be left out.

A branch on the outside of the circuit borders one mesh. Choose the side of that mesh it sits on. A branch between two meshes is shared: choose both meshes and give its side for the lower-numbered one. The page works out the side for the other.

A battery on a left or right side is upright, so its + plate is at the top or the bottom. A battery on a top or bottom side is sideways, so its + plate is at the left or the right. Use one set of units. Ohms and volts give amps.

The page walks round each mesh the way its arrow turns and adds up the voltage changes.

  • Going from the − plate to the + plate of a battery is a rise, written +V. Going from + to − is a drop, written −V.
  • A resistor is always a drop: −R times the current along your walk.
  • In a shared resistor, the neighbouring mesh current adds to yours when it runs the same way you walk, and subtracts when it runs against you.
